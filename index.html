Research does not need money. It needs attention and time.

“Zero Funds, Infinite Potential.”

BORA is a free, volunteer-run research academy that trains Bangladeshi students and early-career scientists to design, run, and publish real research — using open data, open-source tools, and mentorship that costs the learner nothing.

The gap we exist to close

Thousands graduate. Almost none publish.

Bangladesh produces thousands of life-science, medical, veterinary and agricultural graduates every year, yet the overwhelming majority never author a single research paper. The barrier is rarely talent — it is access to equipment, mentorship, journals, and structured training.

Dry-lab research — genomics, AMR surveillance, disease modelling, meta-analysis — needs a laptop, an internet connection, and guidance. We supply the guidance.

Why we exist

Mission & Vision

⚑

Mission

To elevate Bangladesh’s capabilities in biological, medical, and agricultural sciences by providing free, structured, peer-mentored research training to any student with the time and motivation to learn.
✦

Vision

To put Bangladesh’s name on real, citable, internationally visible scientific output — produced by a self-sustaining community of volunteer researchers who once had no other route into research.
How we think

Core Philosophy

Knowledge must remain free and open. By removing the financial barrier to training, we let capability — not funding — decide who gets to do science.

01

Free Access

Every learning resource, tool, and dataset BORA uses must be free and openly licensed. No fees, no paywalls, no hidden costs to any learner — ever.

02

Peer-Powered Mentorship

Every published researcher is expected to mentor the next cohort. Teaching capacity grows with output instead of requiring paid staff.

03

Publish, Don’t Just Learn

Every cohort works toward a real, defensible analysis or manuscript — not tutorials, not certificates of attendance.

The engine

The Mentorship Tree — a 1-to-2 model

Every researcher who joins BORA agrees to one condition: train at least two undergraduate interns, free of charge. When those interns publish their first collaborative work, they become mentors and take on two of their own. Teaching capacity compounds instead of running out.

What we work on

Five research tracks

Deliberately narrow and deliberately local — the fields where Bangladesh generates real genomic and public-health data but has almost no local computational capacity to analyse it.

One Health & Public Health

Human–animal–environment health intersections.

  • Spillover risk mapping
  • Outbreak reconstruction

Infectious Disease Epidemiology

Surveillance, statistics, and mathematical modelling.

  • Trend modelling
  • R₀ estimation

AMR & Genomic Surveillance

Resistance patterns from public genomic repositories.

  • WGS of AMR isolates
  • Resistome mapping

Omics & Bioinformatics

Genomics, transcriptomics, computational biology.

  • RNA-seq reanalysis
  • Variant calling

Veterinary, Agriculture & Fisheries

Food security and animal health through data.

  • Disease-burden mapping
  • Production–export gaps
